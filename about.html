Photo DojoRevival

Snap yourself with a phone or a webcam, then fight a friend sitting next to you on the big screen. A free couch fighting game made of your own photos, in the browser.

  • 👥 2 players, one screen
  • 🎮 Keyboard or controllers
  • 🤖 Or alone vs. the CPU

How it works

  1. Open the game on a laptop or a computer hooked up to a TV, and click “Open the studio”.
  2. Pick a camera. Scan the studio's QR code with a phone and prop it up, and the phone becomes the studio's camera. Or use the computer's own webcam.
  3. Strike 16 poses on the big screen: stance, punches, kicks, getting hit, a victory pose, your fighting style's signature move, and a handshake for good sportsmanship. Then record three battle cries.
  4. Back to the game. Pick your fighter and fight someone in the same room: two players on one keyboard, or with game controllers. Alone? Fight the computer, or practice.

Your photos stay yours

  • The pose detection, the background removal and the voice recording all run on the computer running the studio. Nothing is uploaded for processing.
  • If you use a phone as the camera, its picture goes straight from the phone to that computer over an encrypted peer-to-peer connection (WebRTC). The phone doesn't keep anything. A small server only introduces the two devices; it never sees your photos. On networks that block direct connections, the encrypted video may pass through a relay, which can't read it and doesn't keep it.
  • Fighters are kept in the browser on that computer, and nowhere else. There are no accounts, no cloud storage, no analytics.
  • Delete a fighter from the game's fighter select (click it, then 🗑), or clear the site's data in your browser.

FAQ

Can I play with friends over the internet?

No: it's local multiplayer, a couch game. Both players sit at the same screen, sharing a keyboard or each with a controller. On your own you can fight the computer (easy, normal or hard), practice, or run the tutorial. You can send a friend your fighter file, though, and they can fight as you on their own computer.

What do I need?

A laptop or a computer hooked up to a TV, with a recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. A camera: a phone (any recent iPhone or Android), the computer's webcam, or on a Mac an iPhone through Continuity Camera. Two players can share one keyboard, or plug in game controllers (Xbox, PlayStation and most others work).

Do I have to install anything or sign up?

No. Everything runs in the browser, and there are no accounts.

Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

No. See Your photos stay yours above.

Why does the studio take a while to start the first time?

The computer downloads about 250 MB of AI models the first time (pose detection and background removal). They're stored in the browser, so after that it starts in seconds. The phone downloads nothing: it's only the camera.

My phone won't connect to the studio.

Being on the same Wi-Fi as the computer helps. Some networks (offices, hotels, schools) block direct connections between devices; try another network or a phone hotspot. If the studio page was reloaded, it shows a new code: scan or enter that one. Only one phone can be the camera at a time, and a dropped phone reconnects by itself while its page stays open. You can always use the computer's own webcam instead.

How do I get a clean cut-out?

Stand in front of a plain wall in good, even light, with your whole body in the frame (about 3–4 m from the camera), sideways, facing right. Avoid clothes the same colour as the wall. Any pose can be retaken before you save the fighter, or later with “Edit” on fighter select.

Where are my fighters saved? Can I back them up?

In the browser on the computer that ran the studio and the game. Browsers can clear site data when storage runs low, so use “Export” to keep a .photodojo file of fighters you love. Import it later, on any computer.

Can I build a fighter with just my phone?

No: the AI models need more memory than a phone's browser gives a page, so fighters are built on a computer. The phone can be its camera. Fighter files move between computers with Export and Import.

Is this Nintendo's Photo Dojo?

No. It's an unofficial fan project inspired by Photo Dojo, a 2009 DSiWare game by Nintendo. It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Nintendo, and contains no Nintendo code or assets.

Found a bug? Got an idea?

Tell me on GitHub (a free account is needed). A problem report is filled in with your browser and the game's version to help track it down; you see everything before you send it, and it's public, so leave out anything private.
